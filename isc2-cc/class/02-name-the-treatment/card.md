# Lesson 2. Name the Treatment

**Listen:** name-the-treatment.mp3 (2 min)

**The idea, in three lines**
- A risk is a threat exploiting a vulnerability to harm an asset. Rate it by **likelihood** and **impact**.
- Four treatments: **avoid** (stop the activity or remove the asset), **mitigate** (lower the odds or the damage), **transfer** (insurance, contracts), **accept** (knowingly live with it).
- Accept only with a written reason in the risk register. Transfer moves the cost, never the accountability.

**Impact:** risk scenario questions turn into a quick sort: which of the four boxes is this?

**Who says it:** 4 of the 6 pages on risk management teach likelihood and impact, and 3 of the 6 teach the four treatments; none disagree on the four. They split on acceptance: Aptien calls it the usual choice for small, low risks with no write-up, while Stacker says acceptance with no written "why" is unmanaged risk.

**Try it** · Quick Win · 10 min
On paper, write three risks from your own life (for example: phone stolen, laptop dies, clicking a fake delivery text). For each one:
> **1.** Which treatment are you using today: avoid, mitigate, transfer or accept?
> **2.** One example of it (phone insurance = transfer, a backup = mitigate).
> **3.** If it's accept, one line on why.

**Rule to keep**
Transfer moves the bill, not the blame.
