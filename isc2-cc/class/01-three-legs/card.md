# Lesson 1. Three Legs

**Listen:** three-legs.mp3 (2 min)

**The idea, in three lines**
- **Confidentiality:** only the right people can see it. **Integrity:** it hasn't been changed without authorisation. **Availability:** it's there when the right people need it.
- Every security control protects at least one leg: encryption mainly confidentiality, hashing and signatures integrity, backups and redundancy availability.
- Which leg matters most depends on the setting. Industrial systems often put availability first.

**Impact:** many CC questions become easier once you ask "which leg is at risk here?"

**Who says it:** 5 of the 5 pages on security principles teach the triad this way; none disagree. One page (Coursera) also says to think of confidentiality as privacy; the CC outline treats privacy as its own principle, so keep them separate.

**Try it** · Quick Win · 10 min
On paper, pick three things you use every day (for example your bank app, your work email, your phone photos). For each one:
> **1.** Which leg would hurt most if it failed: confidentiality, integrity or availability?
> **2.** One line on why.
> **3.** One control that protects that leg (a password, a backup, a signature).

**Rule to keep**
For every control, name the leg it protects.
