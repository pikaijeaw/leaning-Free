# Lesson 4. Think Like a Manager

**Listen:** think-like-a-manager.mp3 (2 min)

**The idea, in three lines**
- CC questions ask for the **best** or **first** action. The answer is usually about policy, risk or process, not a clever technical fix.
- The four ISC2 canons, in order: **protect society**, **act honorably**, **provide diligent and competent service to principals**, **advance and protect the profession**.
- When canons conflict, the earlier one wins. Seeing a member breach the Code and not following the complaint procedure can itself be a breach.

**Impact:** when two answers both look right, you have a way to pick the one the exam wants.

**Who says it:** 5 of the 9 pages on exam prep teach the manager mindset, and 3 of the 4 pages on ethics teach the canons in order; none disagree. Training Camp adds that this is why strong technical people sometimes fail the first time.

**Try it** · Quick Win · 10 min
On an index card or a sheet of paper:
> **1.** Write the four canons in order: Society, Honorably, Service, Profession.
> **2.** Under them, write this question: "A contractor's project ended three months ago and they still have admin rights. What do you do first?"
> **3.** Write two answers: what an engineer would type, and what a manager would do first. Circle the one that fixes the cause.

**Rule to keep**
Ask what a manager would do first, not what an engineer would type.
