# Lesson 7. Joiners, Movers, Leavers

**Listen:** joiners-movers-leavers.mp3 (2 min)

**The idea, in three lines**
- Every account has a life: a **joiner** gets access, a **mover** changes role and loses the old role's access, a **leaver** loses all of it. Periodic access reviews and prompt deprovisioning stop privilege creep.
- **Least privilege:** the minimum access the job needs today. **Separation of duties:** no single person completes a sensitive process alone (one creates a payment, another approves it).
- The same lifecycle covers non-human identities: service accounts and AI bots.

**Impact:** "a leaver who kept access" questions become easy marks, and your own shared files get tidier.

**Who says it:** 6 of the 8 pages on access control teach the identity lifecycle; none disagree. 4 of the 8 also teach least privilege and separation of duties. One page (twingate) lists privileged access management (PAM) as an access control model; it's a practice for privileged accounts, not a model.

**Try it** · Admin · 10 min
A leaver review of one thing you own:
> **1.** Open one shared folder or document you own and look at who has access.
> **2.** For each name, ask: does this person still need it today? If not, remove them.
> **3.** For anyone who only needs to read, switch them to view only.

**Rule to keep**
Access ends the day the need ends.
